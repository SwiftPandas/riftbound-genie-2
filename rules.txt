## Quick answers to common questions
- Duel: first to 8 points wins, and you need more points than the opponent (CR 194.2, 472, 485).
- Turn: Awaken (ready everything) -> Beginning (Hold points) -> Channel 2 runes -> Draw 1 -> Main -> End (heal all units, "this turn" effects end, rune pools empty) (CR 315-317).
- Conquer = take control of a battlefield you haven't scored this turn; Hold = still control it in your Beginning Phase. Each battlefield scores you at most once per turn (CR 469-470).
- Final point: the point that would reach 8 by conquering only counts if you have scored every battlefield this turn; otherwise you draw a card instead (CR 471).
- Units enter exhausted unless Accelerate or an effect says otherwise; gear enters ready (CR 143.4, 149.1, 805).
- Lethal damage = at least 1 damage and damage >= Might; Stunned units deal no combat damage (CR 142.4.b, 423).
- Damage heals at the end of every turn and after each combat (CR 143.3.b).
- Tank units must be assigned lethal first; Backline units last (CR 815, 826).
- A spell with an illegal target still resolves for its other parts; a countered card is not "played" (CR 359.3.e, 425).
- Might doesn't decide who controls a battlefield: a unit with 0 Might (e.g. Scuttle Crab) can move onto an empty battlefield, take control (conquer) and hold it.
- Power can come from runes (recycling them), Seals or Gold; using Seal or Gold power first keeps your runes on the board.

## Keywords not in the keyword section (from the cards' own reminder text)
- Deploy: play this card only to a battlefield (not your base). When an opponent holds that battlefield, kill it.
- Disarm X: when this unit attacks, give an enemy unit at that battlefield -X Might this turn (e.g. Ahri, Confident -1; Mysterious Chronomancer -2).

## Detailed notes (Nov 21 pass, written from the full Core Rules, July 16 2026 version)
### 000-099 Golden and Silver rules
- Golden rule (002): if a card truly contradicts the rules, do what the card says.
- Silver rule (051): read card wording through these rules. In card text "card" means a Main Deck card; runes, legends and battlefields aren't "cards" for card effects (052). Units and legends call themselves "I/me", gear and spells "this", battlefields "here" (053).
- "Can't" beats "can" (054): an effect that forbids something wins over one that allows it; "only" limits an action to exactly those circumstances.
- Do as much as you can (055): skip the impossible parts of an effect. A card whose every instruction is impossible still resolves and does nothing.
- Your cards never go to another player's hand, deck, trash, champion zone or banishment (056): they go to the owner's zone instead (e.g. a stolen gear that dies goes to its owner's trash).
### 101-103 Deck building
- A deck is: 1 Champion Legend (sets your Domain Identity, the two domains in its corner), a Main Deck of at least 40 cards including your Chosen Champion, a Rune Deck of exactly 12 runes, and the battlefields your mode needs (3 for a 1v1 duel; no two with the same name).
- Every card must fit your Domain Identity: a one-domain card needs that domain; a multi-domain card needs all its domains in your identity.
- Chosen Champion: a champion unit whose champion tag matches your legend's tag (a Signature unit such as Tibbers can't be it). Any other copy with the same name also counts as your Chosen Champion.
- Up to 3 copies of any named card, champion included (the one in the champion zone counts). Different names are different cards even for the same character.
- At most 3 Signature cards in total, all with your legend's champion tag; Signature cards are never champions.
- Format legality (bans) applies.
### 104-109 Zones
- Board: each player's Base (a location; your permanents and runes live there), the Battlefield Zone (each battlefield is a location) and one Facedown Zone per battlefield.
- Facedown zone: holds 1 card (some effects raise or lower that; extras go to the trash). You can only hide a card there if you control that battlefield. If you lose control of the battlefield, your facedown card there is removed at the next cleanup. Facedown cards are private.
- Legend zone: your Champion Legend can never be removed or moved. Other legends added by effects can be.
- Non-board zones: the Chain (cards being played, public), Trash (public, unordered), Champion Zone (public; your champion can be played from it normally but never returned to it while it holds a card), Main Deck and Rune Deck (order secret), Banishment (public), Hand (private, but the number of cards is public).
- Everything on the board, and its state (buffed, exhausted...), is public.
### 110-116 Setup
- Legend to the legend zone, Chosen Champion to the champion zone, battlefields set aside (1v1: each player brings 3 and one of each player's is used, chosen as the mode says), shuffle both decks, decide the first player at random, each player draws 4, then mulligans in turn order.
### 117-118 Mulligan
- In turn order, each player may set aside up to 2 cards from their opening 4, draws that many, then recycles the set-aside cards (to the bottom of the Main Deck). Then the first player takes the first turn.
### 301-317 The turn, priority and focus
- Game actions happen one at a time; simultaneous ones are ordered by turn order starting with the turn player. A phase ends when the chain is empty and the turn player won't take more actions.
- Four turn states: Neutral Open (no showdown, no chain: by default only the turn player, with priority, can play cards/abilities in their main phase), Neutral Closed (a chain exists: only Reactions), Showdown Open (showdown or combat, no chain: only Action/Reaction, by the player with focus), Showdown Closed (showdown + chain: only Reactions).
- Priority = the right to take an action now: you get it in your main phase when the state is neutral-open, when you gain focus in a showdown, when you control the next chain item after finalizing, or when it's passed to you. Focus = whose go it is in a showdown; gaining focus gives priority; passing priority keeps focus.
- Turn order: Awaken (ready everything you control) → Beginning (start-of-beginning effects, e.g. Temporary units die; then you Hold every battlefield you control = score) → Channel (2 runes from your rune deck, fewer if it's short) → Draw (draw 1; with an empty deck you Burn Out, then still draw 1) → Main (your rune pool empties: unspent energy/power is lost; start-of-main effects; then you act freely; moves can start showdowns and combats) → Ending (end-of-turn effects; then a special cleanup that also heals all units, ends "this turn" effects and empties rune pools; repeats if anything triggered) → next player's turn.
### 407-444 Game actions (exact meanings of card words)
- You only act on your own turn unless a card says otherwise. Draw: top card of your Main Deck to hand (too few cards → Burn Out). Exhaust / Ready: mark a permanent spent / available; an exhaust cost can only be paid if it can actually be done.
- Recycle: put cards on the BOTTOM of the matching deck (several at once go in random order). "Recycle X from [zone]" doesn't target. As an effect, recycle as many as you can.
- Deal (damage), with possible Bonus Damage on top. Heal: remove damage (all units heal at the end of each turn and after each combat).
- Hide: put a card facedown at a battlefield you control; if a facedown card changes zones or the game ends, its owner reveals it.
- Discard: hand → trash without its effect. If told to discard more than you have, discard all.
- Stun: the unit becomes Stunned (a stunned unit deals no combat damage; see the stun notes above). Counter: the card/ability on the chain is cancelled and never counts as played.
- Buff: put a buff counter on a unit (+1 Might; a unit holds at most one; see 701). Banish: straight to the banishment zone (cards can refer to what they banished). Kill: a permanent goes from the board to its owner's trash (lethal damage kills at a cleanup).
- Add: put resources into your rune pool; resource-adding abilities resolve immediately and can't be responded to, and Reaction add-abilities can be used whenever you need to pay. Channel: runes from the top of your rune deck onto the board (as many as possible).
- Burn Out: if you must take more cards from your Main Deck than it has, you burn out (see the burn-out notes above: shuffle your trash in as your new deck and your opponent gains a point), then continue. Burn X: top X cards of your Main Deck to your trash; if you run out mid-burn, burn out, then burn the rest.
- Double: add its current value again. Swap: exchange two numbers (e.g. two units' Might). Attach / Detach: equipment linked to a unit moves with it; attaching doesn't target by itself.
- Predict: look at the top card of your Main Deck and choose whether to recycle it (Predict X = do it X times, as many as the deck allows). Prevent: reduce damage a unit would take by a tracked amount; fully prevented damage counts as not dealt.
- Replace X with Y: a token takes X's place keeping its effects/statuses; X goes to banishment (a replaced token just stops existing); it can later be "swapped back". Create: makes a new object (owned by its creator) where the effect says.
- Empower / Disempower: give or remove the Empowered status. Skip: an event is replaced with nothing. Pay: remove a resource from your rune pool (optional "pay" instructions can be declined).
### 445-458 Movement and recalls
- A move is a permanent going from one board location to another. It's instant, doesn't use the chain and can't be reacted to (you react to what it causes). Changing zones isn't a move; equipment travelling with its unit isn't a move.
- Standard move (a unit's built-in ability): base → a battlefield, or battlefield → base; Ganking also allows battlefield → battlefield. Effects can move units too, with their own limits. Moving to an uncontested battlefield you don't control makes it Contested (→ a showdown if empty, combat if enemies are there). A cleanup follows every move.
- Recall = sent to its base without moving: doesn't trigger "when I move", can't be stopped by movement restrictions, and keeps damage and statuses unless the recall says otherwise. Gear left at a battlefield is recalled at the next cleanup.
### 318-324 Cleanups (the game's automatic tidy-up)
- A cleanup happens after almost any change: a phase change, something added to or finished on the chain, cards entering or leaving the board, a status change, a completed move, or the game opening or closing. Nothing on the chain resolves during a cleanup, and no cleanup happens mid-resolution (it waits). Cleanups repeat until nothing changes.
- Order inside a cleanup: (1) a player at or above the victory score (8 in a duel) with more points than every opponent wins; (2) attacker/defender labels are fixed for units at the combat's battlefield (units there take their controller's role; units elsewhere lose the role); (3a) units with lethal damage trigger their Deathknell / "when I die" abilities, then (3b) they die and go to their owners' trash; (4) in an open turn state, with no showdown or combat there, a player who has no units at a battlefield they control loses control of it; (5) gear and runes that ended up at a battlefield or in someone else's base go home, and hidden cards at a battlefield you don't control go to the trash; (6) a showdown becomes staged at each contested battlefield; (7) a combat becomes staged where opposing units share a contested battlefield; (8) contested status is removed where the contesting player no longer has units and nothing is ongoing; (9) in a neutral open state the turn player picks a staged showdown to start, (10) or a staged combat to start (a combat staged where a non-combat showdown is running turns that showdown into a combat showdown).
### 325-348 Chains and showdowns (when you can respond)
- Two windows to act outside your main phase: during a chain, and during a showdown.
- The chain: whenever a card is played or an ability activated, it goes on the chain (only one chain at a time; anything played while one exists joins it). While a chain exists the turn is "closed": by default nothing can be played except Reactions (cards or abilities with [Reaction], or that gain it).
- Processing order (HOT FEPR): first handle outstanding tasks (cleanups etc.), then Finalize (pending items are finished being played, oldest first; units, gear and resource-adding abilities resolve immediately once finalized, they can't be responded to), Execute (the player with priority may play a legally timed card/ability, which goes on top, or pass), Pass (priority goes round in turn order; when everyone passes in a row without adding anything) and Resolve (the newest item resolves fully). Then the next player with priority can respond again. So the last thing played resolves first.
- When the chain empties the turn is open again. If that happened during a showdown, focus passes to the next player, unless the chain was started by a triggered ability or a resource-adding ability.
- If nothing is pending, no tasks remain and no showdown/combat is on: in the main phase the turn player gets priority; in other phases the game moves on.
- Showdowns: a window where players take turns ("focus") playing Action/Reaction cards in an open state, each starting its own chain. During a showdown (or combat) the turn is in a "showdown state": by default only cards with [Action] or [Reaction] can be played.
- A showdown starts during a cleanup when a battlefield is contested in a neutral open turn state. Moving into an empty enemy-controlled battlefield gives a non-combat showdown; if opposing units are both there it opens as the first step of combat (an existing showdown there becomes a combat showdown).
- The player who contested the battlefield gets focus first. With focus you may play a legal card/ability (focus passes after its chain closes) or pass. When every player passes in a row, the showdown closes: a combat showdown goes on to combat damage; a non-combat showdown gives control to the only player with units left there, which is a conquer if they haven't scored that battlefield this turn.
### 349-355 Playing a card: steps and targeting
- A card counts as "played" only once the whole process finishes (a countered card was never played). Tokens can be played too. Permanents become game objects; spells do their effect and go to the trash.
- Steps: (1) move the card to the chain (the turn becomes closed; finish any resolving effect or tasks first); (2) make choices; (3) total cost; (4) legality; (5) pay; then it's finalized.
- Choices made while playing: "as I am played" choices and whether to pay optional additional costs; where a unit enters (by default your base or a battlefield you control; some effects allow others); modes; move destinations; and targets. Triggered abilities choose their targets later, when they trigger.
- What counts as a target: anything an effect specifically chooses on the board, on the chain, in a public zone (trash, legend/champion zone, facedown zone) or a player/zone. Not targets: cards in hidden zones (your hand), things only named as a restriction ("a unit at a battlefield" targets the unit, not the battlefield; "all units at a battlefield" targets the battlefield), things that are part of a cost or trigger condition, things selected automatically ("kill all units at battlefields" targets nothing), things other players pick, and "you must" instructions.
- "Can't be chosen by enemy spells and abilities" means the unit is never a legal enemy target. A spell can't target itself, but a permanent's ability can target that permanent.
- Group targets ("units with total Might 4 or less"): if the group stops fitting before resolution, the caster picks a fitting subset of the original targets.
- "Any number" / "up to" allows zero targets. Split damage: each unit you pick is a target, at most one target per point of damage; the split is decided on resolution, each target must get at least 1; if there are more targets than damage left, the caster drops the fewest needed.
- Choices are locked after this step unless an effect says to choose again. You can't choose something that's certain to make the play illegal later.
- Costs: base cost first ("play for [X]" replaces it; "ignoring its cost" sets energy and power to 0; "ignoring its Energy cost" only the energy), then additional costs: mandatory ones ("as an additional cost", no "may"; Deflect's extra cost is one) and optional ones you chose in step 2 (e.g. Accelerate's cost still applies even if the base cost is ignored).
### 358-359 Legality and resolving
- Legality check: the card must be legally timed now (closed chain or showdown → only Reaction / Action as allowed). An effect that forbids a game action doesn't stop you playing a card that would do it; that part is just skipped on resolution. If any check fails, everything is undone.
- Permanents: once finalized they leave the chain, their abilities turn on and their text executes top to bottom. Units enter EXHAUSTED at the chosen location; non-unit gear enters READY at your base.
- Spells: stay on the chain; other players may react before it resolves; then the effect happens top to bottom and the card goes to its owner's trash.
- Illegal targets: the spell still resolves. A target is illegal if it no longer fits the targeting text (e.g. no longer an enemy, no longer at a battlefield, Might now above the limit, no longer exhausted) or if it left the board and came back (it's a new object). Instructions about illegal targets are skipped; with several targets, only the legal ones are affected; if every instruction's targets are gone the spell does nothing but still counts as played.
- Information about a gone or illegal target is "null" (e.g. its Might counts as nothing, so "deal damage equal to its Might" deals none).
- A spell can "look back" at a card it moved as a cost or effect. Linked instructions: if the first part didn't happen, the dependent later part doesn't either.
- Words like "here", "my", "its" are checked when that instruction executes (e.g. if a unit is moved back to base before its attack trigger resolves, "here" is no longer the battlefield and the trigger misses; a Might reduced in response changes the damage dealt).
- "Friendly"/"enemy" in a triggered ability is checked when the instruction runs.
### 459-466 Combat step by step
- Combat happens during a cleanup when the chain is empty, a combat is staged (units of two opposing players at one battlefield) and nothing else is going on elsewhere. If several battlefields have combats staged, the turn player picks the order. Only two players can ever fight at one battlefield.
- Step 1, combat showdown: "start of combat/showdown" effects happen; the player whose units contested the battlefield is the Attacker (the other is the Defender) and all their units there get that role (units arriving later get it at the next cleanup). The attacker gets focus. Triggered abilities from attacking/defending go on the chain: attacker's first, defender's last. Then players alternate playing Action/Reaction cards as in any showdown.
- Step 2, combat damage (only if both sides still have units there): each side totals the current Might of its units; starting with the attacker, each assigns that much damage among the other side's units, then all damage is dealt at once. Assigning rules: a unit must be given lethal damage in full before you move to the next; you can't give a unit more than lethal unless no units are left; obey Tank/Backline-type requirements; a unit that can't be damaged never needs lethal assigned. Lethal = at least 1 damage and at least its Might.
- Step 3, resolution: a combat cleanup kills units with lethal damage, then HEALS all units, then RECALLS the attackers (sends them back to base) if any defenders are still alive there. Result: the player who alone has units left wins the combat, the other loses; "no result" if attackers were recalled, both or neither have units. The player left at the battlefield establishes control (a conquer if they haven't scored it this turn; it can even be the defender, who keeps it without conquering if they already controlled it); contested status clears; enemy hidden cards there are removed. Then "this combat" effects end and "end of combat" effects happen.
### 467-472 Scoring
- Conquer: you gain control of a battlefield you haven't scored this turn. Hold: you still control a battlefield in your Beginning Phase. Each battlefield can score you only once per turn (by either method), and its conquer/hold abilities trigger only then.
- Each score gives 1 point, except the final point by conquering: if you're 1 point from winning (7 in a duel), a conquer gives the final point only if you have scored every battlefield this turn; otherwise you draw a card instead. Points from other sources (hold, card effects) aren't limited this way.
- You win at a cleanup when you have at least the victory score (8 in a duel) and more points than every opponent.
### 805-829 Keywords in full
- Accelerate: as you play the unit you may pay an extra [1] energy plus 1 power of one of the unit's domains (any domain if it has none); if you do it enters READY instead of exhausted (it never "becomes ready", so ready-triggers don't fire). Can't be paid once it's on the board; duplicates do nothing extra.
- Action: permission only. The card or ability can also be played/activated during showdowns, on anyone's turn (as well as its normal timing). It doesn't change what the card does.
- Reaction: everything Action allows, plus it can be played while a chain is open (closed state), on anyone's turn, i.e. in response to other cards. A unit with Reaction still has to be played to your base or a battlefield you control.
- Assault X (X=1 if blank): +X Might while it is an attacker. Several Assault values add up.
- Shield X (X=1 if blank): +X Might while it is a defender. Several Shield values add up.
- Deathknell: "when I die, [effect]". It triggers only if the unit is killed and actually goes to the trash (if the death is replaced, e.g. by a recall, the trigger is removed). The game notes its location and stats just before it goes, so the effect still knows them. Each Deathknell triggers separately; you order them.
- Deflect X (X=1 if blank): opponents' spells and abilities that choose it cost X more power (any domain) for each time they choose it. Values add up.
- Ganking: the unit's standard move may also go battlefield → battlefield.
- Hidden: on your turn in an open state, while the card is in your hand (or champion zone) you may pay [Rainbow] (1 power of any domain) to hide it facedown at a battlefield you control that has no facedown card. Hiding doesn't open a chain. From the next turn it has Reaction and can be played for free (ignoring its base cost) while you still control that battlefield. Played from hidden: a permanent must be played to that battlefield (gear too), and its targets and any unit it plays must be at that battlefield; a spell with no legal targets there can't be played from hidden. Instead of hiding, you can also just play it normally for its cost.
- Legion: the text after it is active only if you've already played another card this turn (one other card satisfies every Legion you have).
- Tank: must be assigned lethal combat damage before any of its controller's non-Tank units. With several Tanks, any order among them.
- Temporary: killed at the start of its controller's Beginning Phase, before scoring (it can't score a hold).
- Vision: "when this is played, predict" (look at the top card of your Main Deck; you may recycle it). Several Visions trigger separately.
- Equip [cost]: "[cost]: attach this gear to a unit you control" (the unit is a target). The unit is "equipped" while any Equipment is attached. Each Equip ability can be used separately.
- Quick-Draw: the Equipment has Reaction and attaches itself to a unit you control when played.
- Repeat [cost]: an optional extra cost; if paid, the spell/ability's instructions run once more on resolution (choices for the repeat are made while playing and may differ). Each Repeat can be paid once; the card is still only played once.
- Weaponmaster: when you play this unit, you may pick an Equipment you control and pay its Equip cost minus [Rainbow] to attach it to this unit, even outside normal timing (if the cost can't be paid or it can't move, it stays put).
- Ambush: the unit may be played to a battlefield where you have units, and has Reaction while played there (so it can come down mid-combat). If your units leave before it finishes being played, that location is no longer valid.
- Hunt X: when this unit conquers or holds, you gain X XP. Values add up.
- Level N: the text after it works while you have N or more XP (it switches off as soon as you drop below N; it follows the current controller's XP).
- Unique: deck-building only, at most 1 copy of that name (Signature + Unique: still 3 Signatures total, 1 of each Unique name).
- Backline: must be assigned lethal combat damage after all of its controller's non-Backline units.
- Empower [cost]: "[cost]: Empower this; only if not already Empowered". Empowered [text]: that text works while it has Empowered status; "when I become Empowered" triggers then.
- Flow [cost]: you may play the spell from your trash for its Flow cost (same timing rules as normal), then it's banished instead of going back to the trash.
